## WHAT-WHY-HOW-IF model

**- Will the paper present a model or a method?** Let's call this a basic mental
gadget, i.e., probably closer to "model". It is about seeing four different
aspects when dealing, for example, with source code comments or formal
requirements. Whether or not WWHI can dare to become a method, is to be seen.
**- What's the target audience? Systems engineers from regulated industry
(knowing and liking SEBoK, ISO 29148, ISTQB, ...), or common Dev commenting
code?** I would say it is both, depending on how well this model can be refined.
The home territory is regulated industries though but let's if we can advance to
the worlds where there are no formal requirements. **- Does the paper have
sympathy for, say, V-model? Or is it ok with chaotic development as long as
eventually there's some WWHY documented?** Even if a development is chaotic,
there have to be people who carry WHAT in their mind in one way or the other.
**- "Why's" in practice are not independent from solution.** This is true. The
model is, of course, not trying to be The Model of the Entire World. Even if the
solution is coupled with WHYs, the model is there to help us realize that the
coupling is happening. **- Prescribing "shall" and formal requirements excludes
agile projects (AI search claims \~80% percent of today's projects are agile).**
We can relax "shall"s there but it should be mentioned somewhere that if
"shall"s are used, they belong to WHAT and nowhere else. A special case:
Stakeholder Requirements from the Problem space. Let's ask in return: What is
WHAT in the Agile world? **- Concrete realization may come before formulating
system obligations: Let's explore what's possible, then find someone to want
it.** Suggestion: Maybe these are Stakeholder Requirements that can be
formulated by customers even without the system to exist yet? **- What is "naive
development"?** Not enough thought was given to this yet. Naive is probably the
same as what was referred to as "chaotic" above.
